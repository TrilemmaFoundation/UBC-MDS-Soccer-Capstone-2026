# Code of Conduct: DSCI 591 Capstone

**Project:** Delivering Elite European Football (Soccer) Analytics  
**Effective Date:** April 26, 2026  
**Team Members:** Li Pu, Ngoc Minh Quan Hoang, Rabindranath Duran Pons, Teem Kwong

---

## 1. Purpose
This Code of Conduct establishes shared standards for how our team members engage, communicate, and behave toward one another throughout the DSCI 591 Capstone project. It aims to foster a collaborative, inclusive, and respectful environment where all members can contribute their best work. This document is adapted from the UBC MDS Code of Conduct and the Open Source Guide.

## 2. Our Pledge
In the interest of creating an open and welcoming environment, we as team members pledge to make participation in our project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## 3. Expected Behavior
All team members are expected to:
* Be respectful of differing viewpoints and experiences. Disagreements about technical decisions are normal and healthy; personal attacks are not.
* Use welcoming and inclusive language in all communications (Slack, GitHub, meetings).
* Give and receive constructive feedback gracefully. Focus on the work, not the person.
* Accept responsibility for mistakes and learn from them openly.
* Prioritize the success of the team over individual recognition.
* Be honest about progress, blockers, and capacity; ask for help early rather than falling behind.
* Respect each other's time by being punctual to meetings and responsive on Slack within 24 hours.
* Support teammates who are struggling before their tasks become blockers for the team.

## 4. Unacceptable Behavior
The following behaviors are not acceptable in any team interaction:
* Personal insults, derogatory comments, or attacks on character.
* Harassment of any kind, including unwelcome comments related to personal characteristics.
* Dismissing or belittling a teammate's contributions or ideas.
* Repeatedly failing to meet commitments without communication or effort to resolve.
* Taking credit for work done by another team member.
* Deliberately withholding information or resources needed to complete work.
* Any form of academic dishonesty as outlined in the UBC MDS policies.

## 5. Collaboration Standards

### 5.1 Technical Disagreements
When team members disagree on a technical approach, the following process applies:
1. Each person states their position with reasoning (prioritizing data or evidence over opinion).
2. The team discusses trade-offs openly.
3. If consensus cannot be reached, the owner of the relevant workstream makes the final call.
4. The decision is documented in a GitHub Issue for future reference.

### 5.2 Workload Imbalance
* If any team member feels the workload is unevenly distributed, they should raise the concern at the next team meeting or via Slack.
* The team will discuss and adjust assignments; no member should feel pressured to stay silent about workload concerns.

## 6. Reporting and Resolution
If a member experiences or witnesses behavior that violates this Code of Conduct:
* **Step 1 (Direct conversation):** If comfortable, address the behavior directly with the person involved in a private, respectful manner.
* **Step 2 (Team discussion):** If the behavior continues or the member is not comfortable with a direct conversation, raise the issue at a team meeting.
* **Step 3 (Escalation):** If internal resolution fails, the team will contact the mentor for mediation and support.

## 7. Academic Integrity
All team members agree to uphold UBC's academic integrity standards and the MDS Code of Conduct. Specifically:
* All code, analysis, and written content submitted must be the original work of the team or properly attributed to external sources.
* Use of generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) is permitted for coding assistance, but all members must fully understand and be able to explain any AI-assisted code they submit.
* Open-source libraries and external resources must be credited in the project README and documentation.

## 8. Amendments
This Code of Conduct is a living document. Any team member may propose amendments at any time. Changes require agreement from all four members and should be documented with the date of revision.
